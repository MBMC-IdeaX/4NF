# Onboarding a bus company: what the law asks, what happens in practice, and what Bhada should do

Date: 3 Oct 2026. Status: research and proposal for the owner's decision. Not legal advice — every point
marked **confirm** needs a Nepali transport/payments lawyer before go-live.

## 1. What the law asks

### The operator
- Public transport is run by **companies**. Since the 30 Mar 2018 amendment of the Transport Management
  Directive 2004, DoTM gives route permits only to transport companies registered under the Companies Act
  and registered with DoTM, not to committees or individuals; 113 private limited companies were registered
  with DoTM at that point. ([Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/business/registration-at-dotm-must-to-avail-new-vehicle-route-permits),
  [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2018/04/05/five-new-firms-get-permits-to-ply-buses-on-dhulikhel-route))
- Committees still exist. The Supreme Court (Apr 2024, published Oct 2024) struck down the 2018 ban on
  committee registration renewals; committees can renew under the Organisation Registration Act at the
  District Administration Office. ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/10/04/court-scraps-six-year-old-ban-on-transport-company-registrations-and-renewals))
- Papers a route-permit application carries, as reported by practitioners: company registration certificate
  (Office of Company Registrar), PAN/VAT certificate, tax clearance, directors' citizenship, and per vehicle
  its registration (bluebook), insurance and drivers' licences. **confirm** against the current Directive.
  ([CompanyNP](https://companynp.com/public-transport-business-registration-in-nepal/),
  [Lawsagar](https://lawsagar.com/2025/10/03/transport-and-logistics-business-licenses-nepal/))

### The route
- DoTM designates and publishes routes; no public vehicle may run a designated route without a **route
  permit**, applied for by the owner or manager with fees. Permits have been issued for limited periods
  (four months at a time historically) and renewed. ([Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act 2049](https://www.nepalarchives.com/content/motor-vehicles-and-transport-management-act-2049-1993/))
- In Bagmati, a Province Transport Operation and Management Board also handles urban route permits and fare
  setting; DoTM has at times stopped new permits in the Valley for congestion. **confirm** which office
  issues the permits for each route Bhada serves.
  ([nepalindata route procedures](http://nepalindata.com/media/items/15/Task-A4_ROUTE_APPROVAL_AND_PERMIT_PROCEDURES.pdf))
- Long-distance operators must register vehicles in DoTM's online ticket monitoring system (Online Ticket
  Purchase and Sale Directive 2077). ([Fiscal Nepal](https://www.fiscalnepal.com/2020/11/20/2330/dotm-begins-online-ticketing-monitoring-system/))

### The bus
- **Bluebook** (vehicle registration certificate) from the Transport Management Office; renewed with the
  **annual vehicle tax** (paid to the province) and a renewal fee. ([Khatapana](https://khatapana.com/blogs/379/how-to-renew-your-vehicles-bluebook-in-nepal-a-complete-guide))
- **Third-party insurance is compulsory**; a bluebook cannot be renewed without it. ([eSewa blog](https://blog.esewa.com.np/bluebook-renewal-and-third-party-insurance-services),
  [Direction Kathmandu](https://directionkathmandu.com/blogs-news/renew-your-car-bike-bluebook-in-less-than-15-minutes-nepal))
- **Pollution (emission) test** — the green sticker — is part of renewal. ([Khatapana](https://khatapana.com/blogs/379/how-to-renew-your-vehicles-bluebook-in-nepal-a-complete-guide))
- **Route permit** for the route it runs (above).

### The people
- **Driving licence category G** for buses (F for minibuses); heavy categories need age 21+.
  ([Wikipedia, plates/licences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Nepal),
  [Meroauto](https://www.en.meroauto.com/provincial-governments-set-different-drivers-license-fees-across-nepal/))
- **Labour Act 2074**: a **written employment contract** for every employee except casual work; minimum wage
  NPR 19,550/month from 1 Shrawan 2082; 8 h/day, 48 h/week, overtime ×1.5.
  ([Lawneeti](https://lawneeti.com/publications/key-insights-of-the-labor-act-2074),
  [Notary Nepal](https://notarynepal.com/blog/minimum-wage-nepal))

### Bhada itself
- **Money**: holding customer money needs an NRB PSP licence (Payment and Settlement Act 2075); a PSP must
  have a settlement bank. Bhada works through a licensed partner. ([Lawin](https://lawinpartners.com/publication/digital-wallet-regulations-in-nepal),
  [Law Imperial](https://www.lawimperial.com/registration-of-payment-service-providers/))
- **Contracts online**: contracts formed electronically are valid under the Electronic Transactions Act 2063
  with clear offer and acceptance; a digital signature equals a wet signature only with a certificate from a
  licensed certifying authority. ([Notary Nepal](https://notarynepal.com/blog/electronic-transactions-act-nepal))
- **Personal data**: Privacy Act 2075 — informed, specific consent stating purpose, scope, recipients and
  retention; use only for the stated purpose. ([Privacy Act text](https://hmis.gov.np/media/22/03_The-Privacy-Act-2075-2018.pdf))
- **Tax**: Bhada's fees carry VAT (13%) once registered; a company paying a VAT-registered service provider
  withholds TDS at 1.5% of the service fee. ([Bizsewa](https://bizsewa.com/tax-deducted-source-tds-rates-nepal/)) **confirm**.

### From the primary texts (Law Commission of Nepal)

Read in full or in the relevant chapters, from lawcommission.gov.np:

- **Urban Area Public Transport (Management) Authority Act 2079** (Act 25 of 2079, authenticated
  2079-06-09). [link](https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13312/urban-area-public-transport--management--authority-act-/)
  - §2(ठ): a *service provider* is a **firm or company** permitted under law to run public transport in the
    authority's area.
  - §3, §12–13: a province may set up a public transport authority for urban areas. A **Federal Capital
    authority** for Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur is chaired by the Kathmandu mayor. Its council includes
    DoTM, the Bagmati transport ministry, traffic police, three large operators, consumer, worker, senior
    and disabled representatives.
  - §5(1): the authority **sets routes and fare rates** in urban areas (ण), sets service conditions (त) and
    vehicle standards (थ), develops an **integrated, cashless electronic fare system** (ङ), sets the
    **cashless e-ticket format** (ट), issues electronic tickets (ठ), runs **real-time service information**
    (च), collects and keeps transport data (छ), and may **buy services from or contract the private
    sector** (ध).
  - §16(थ): the chief executive **distributes fare revenue among service providers per agreement**.
  - §18: fines of Rs 1,000–5,000 for travelling without a ticket or without paying the set fare (घ), and for
    **charging more or less than the set fare** (ङ).
  - §20: every service provider needs the **authority's approval before running vehicles** in its area;
    those already running had six months to get it. The authority keeps the list.
- **Payment and Settlement Act 2075** (Act 31 of 2075). [link](https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/12215/12215-payment-and-settlement-act-20/)
  - §2: a *payment service provider* includes anyone who pays amounts between itself and customers for
    goods or services, or runs electronic payment transactions; it must be a **company** licensed under §9.
  - §5 and §36(क): acting as a payment system operator or PSP **without a licence is an offence**. This
    confirms Bhada must not hold or move customer money itself.
- **Privacy Act 2075** (Act 14 of 2075). [link](https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/12261/12261-the-privacy-act-2075/)
  - §12(2)–(3): **consent** is required to collect personal data, and data a body corporate collects may be
    used **only for the purpose it was collected for**.
  - §12(4)(b), (f), (h): **income, signatures/e-signatures and business transactions** may not be given to
    anyone without consent. That covers a rider's trips and an owner's takings.
  - §19: privacy of information held electronically.
- Also downloaded for the next pass: Consumer Protection Act 2075 and the Motor Vehicle and Transport
  Management Rules 2054. The Motor Vehicles Act 2049 itself sits on repository.lawcommission.gov.np, which
  refused connections from this machine; its chapters on registration (3), licences (4), route permits (6)
  and insurance (8) are summarised from secondary sources above.

## 2. What happens in practice

- The permit holder is the **transport company** (or, still, a committee). Many buses belong to **individual
  owners** who run under that company and pay it a levy; the company deals with DoTM.
- New companies that won competitive permits in 2018 (Mayur Yatayat, Kalanki–Banepa) faced blockades from
  committees. Any rollout has to make the incumbents better off, not threaten them.
  ([Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/as-transport-cartel-intensifies-protests-mayur-yatayat-holds-its-ground/))
- Owners renew bluebook, tax, insurance and pollution yearly at offices or through eSewa; a lapse means fines
  and a bus off the road.
- Crews are mostly paid daily in cash out of the day's takings; written contracts are rare.

## 3. Proposal: onboard the permit holder, serve every bus owner under it

**Who signs up**: the **transport company or committee** — the body that holds the route permits — not a lone
owner. Bhada onboards it in person (admin side), once:

1. **Agreement first.** A Bhada onboarding officer meets the company, explains the terms, and both sign:
   - the **service agreement** (plan: per-bus monthly or growth share; fees; data; support; exit);
   - the **payout mandate** (the partner pays the company's named account; Bhada fees are deducted);
   - a **data consent** notice under the Privacy Act (what is collected, why, who sees it, how long).
   Signed on paper and scanned, or accepted in-app with OTP as an electronic contract; the evidence is kept.
2. **Company papers checked against originals**: OCR registration, PAN/VAT, tax clearance, directors'
   citizenship, DoTM transport-company registration.
3. **Buses entered by Bhada from the papers**: plate and owner from the bluebook, route from the route permit
   (the route is built by Bhada's reviewer from the permit — never typed by an owner), insurance, tax and
   pollution dates recorded with their expiry.
4. **Each member bus owner** is invited to see their own bus or buses: today's fares, payouts, renewals.
5. **Drivers and conductors**: licence category G/F checked; Bhada generates the Labour-Act written contract
   from a template for the company to sign with each crew member.

**Why each party is better off**

| Party | Today | With Bhada |
|---|---|---|
| Rider | Change, haggling, overcharging on short hops | Pays by the km, offline, with a receipt |
| Bus owner | Takings in cash, counted at night, leakage | Per-bus earnings to the rupee, paid to their bank; renewal reminders before a fine |
| Transport company / committee | Levy collected in cash; paperwork by hand | Levy deducted automatically and transparently per bus; every bus's papers and expiries on one screen; the DoTM return produced for them |
| Conductor and driver | Cash wages, no contract | A written contract the app produced, clean-trip bonus, a record of their work |
| Regulator | Little data | Overload register and daily return from settled rides |
| Bhada | — | Per-bus monthly fee or a growth share; no money held |
| Payment partner | — | Ride and top-up volume |

The levy is the hinge: the company keeps the role it has today — and is paid more reliably — so it has
reason to bring its member buses instead of resisting.

**What the Owner app becomes**: view and act, not data entry. Today; buses (read-only facts, request a new
bus or a change); papers (upload a **renewal**, see expiries); people (invite crew, contracts); money
(balance, payouts, statement, levy). Registration and verification move to the admin console.

### The bigger win: the authority

The Authority Act gives the urban transport authority the duty to build a cashless e-fare system, set
routes and fares, keep transport data and share fare revenue among operators. Those are Bhada's parts.
Offering Bhada **to the authority** (§5(1)(ध) lets it contract the private sector) turns a fight with
committees into a mandate:

- **Authority**: meets its statutory duties without building software; sees every bus live.
- **Operators**: one system the regulator already accepts; a fare that is always the set fare, so the §18
  fine for charging more or less never applies.
- **Bhada**: one contract covering the valley, plus the per-bus operator fee.

Two consequences for the build:
- Bhada's fare table must be **the authority's or DoTM's published fare**, not Bhada's own. Tariffs are
  already add-only rows (`TARIFFS`, `tariffs` table); each needs the notice it came from. The
  `NPR-KTM-2026` demo tariff must never price a live fare.
- The ticket format must follow whatever e-ticket format the authority sets under §5(1)(ट). BT1 and BM1
  are versioned, so a new format is a new version, not a rewrite.

### Reading the permit to build the route

When a reviewer opens a request, the uploaded route permit is read automatically. A vision model extracts
the route name, plate, permit number, validity dates and the named stops, and fills in the route builder.
The reviewer checks each field against the image and approves. A person still decides; the machine saves
the typing and catches a plate or a date that doesn't match the bus. Needs an API key and a Privacy Act
data-processing note, because the permit carries the owner's name.
